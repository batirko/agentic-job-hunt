# Interview Feedback Log

Every piece of post-interview feedback received from a company, recorded verbatim, plus what it actually means and what changed because of it.

**Why this file exists:** rejection feedback is the only external, unfiltered read on how the interview performance lands. It arrives once, gets read once, and is normally lost. Recorded here it compounds: four vague-feeling data points from four companies turn out to be one specific, coachable pattern.

**How to use it:** read the Pattern Synthesis before any interview prep, then go straight to [seniority-playbook.md](seniority-playbook.md) section 13, which is where the corrective lives. Any new feedback gets appended as an entry, then the synthesis gets re-derived.

**Canonical answer structure:** the six-beat shape in [seniority-playbook.md](seniority-playbook.md) section 13. That is the single source of truth. This file explains why it looks the way it does.

## Two standing rules for every interview transcript 

### Rule 1: sweep the transcript for profile intel before analysing performance

A transcript is two things at once: a record of how the call went, and the only place where you tell your own stories out loud, unrehearsed, with details that are not written down anywhere. The second one is the part that gets lost, because the analysis instinct goes straight to delivery.

**Every transcript gets swept for material that belongs in a permanent file, before or alongside the performance read:**

| What to look for | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| A story told with a beat that is not in the story bank (a named counterparty argument, a cost, a metric, an honest coda) | [story-bank.md](story-bank.md), as an amendment or a new entry |
| A metric, number or measurement name said out loud for the first time | story-bank.md, and check it against the attribution discipline note |
| A preference, deal-breaker, or working principle stated to an interviewer | `config/profile.yml` or `modes/_profile.md` |
| A company fact, org detail, or strategy statement from the interviewer's side | the company's interview-prep doc |
| A commitment the company made on the record | the company's prep doc and the tracker note |

**Why this rule exists.** It has already paid for itself. In one engineer call, three things were said out loud that existed nowhere else in writing: the objection a neighbouring team had raised against a platform, the legacy constraint that actually decided the design, and the adoption metrics used to verify it. All of it was depth-2 and depth-3 material for a story already in the bank, and without the sweep all of it would have died with the transcript.

### Rule 2: grade the delivery A to F, every time

The performance read used to be a table of answer durations, which measures one failure mode and misses the rest. Every transcript now gets a grade, per dimension and overall, so the trend is legible across calls instead of re-derived each time.

| Dimension | What an A looks like |
|---|---|
| **Opening** | The interviewer never has to ask what the product was, who it was for, or what the company does |
| **Compression** | No answer runs past 90 seconds unless depth was explicitly invited. Nothing gets cut off for time |
| **Question fidelity** | The asked question is answered first. Zero redirects needed |
| **Decision visibility** | The rejected option is named unprompted, in the same sentence as the decision. "I" attached to calls and mistakes |
| **Calibration** | Uncertainty stated once, plainly. Never a good answer bracketed by two disclaimers |
| **Questions asked** | Extracted something the prep docs could not have known |
| **Signals received** | Unprompted validation from the interviewer |

**Grade bands:**

- **A**: would advance on delivery alone, independent of content.
- **B**: content carried it. One confirmed failure mode appeared and was recovered.
- **C**: the interviewer had to work to follow. Two or more failure modes, or one that consumed real clock.
- **D**: needed rescuing. A fair, well-telegraphed question went effectively unanswered.
- **F**: lost the thread, or lost the room.

## Entries

> One entry per call, newest first. Record the date, company, stage, interviewer,
> and how you obtained the record (full transcript, interviewer audio only, or
> reconstructed from memory). Then the intel harvested, the seven dimension
> grades with the specific moment behind each, and the overall grade.
>
> When a company states a rejection reason, record their **exact words**.
> Paraphrase loses the diagnosis. Feedback across three or four processes tends
> to converge on one thing, and that convergence is the most valuable output
> this whole system produces.
