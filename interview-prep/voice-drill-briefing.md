# Voice Drill Briefing

Reading and writing cannot fix delivery. This is the one prep item that needs
saying things out loud, timed, and recorded.

Paste section A into a voice-capable assistant, run section C, then ask for the
debrief in section D verbatim.

**Before you start, test whether it actually read the brief.** Ask it to repeat
back the feedback pattern you pasted. If it cannot, the context did not land.
Repaste it.

## A. Paste this in

> **Who I am:** _(a 60-second factual version of your background: current scope
> and who your users are, the two roles before it, and the three or four
> verified outcomes you can cite. The drill partner needs this to probe you, not
> to flatter you.)_

> **Who you are playing:** _(the real interviewer if you know them: title,
> career arc, what they are technically fluent in, what they are likely to be
> skeptical about, and whether the level is already fixed or will be set by this
> conversation. If you do not know them yet, name the stage instead: recruiter,
> hiring manager, peer/principal, founder, or panel, and use the interviewer
> role map in [seniority-playbook.md](seniority-playbook.md) section 8.)_

> **Why we are doing this:** _(paste the actual feedback you have received,
> verbatim, from [interview-feedback-log.md](interview-feedback-log.md). If
> several processes converge on one thing, name it. If you have no feedback yet,
> say so and ask to be graded cold against the seven dimensions in the feedback
> log.)_

> **The rules.** Ask one question, then stop and let me answer out loud. Do not
> coach mid-answer. Push back at least once per answer, the way a skeptical
> interviewer would. Time every answer and tell me the duration afterwards.

## B. Probe bank

Pull from the four probe domains in the playbook: prioritization, customers and
problems, stakeholders and influence, and failure. Add whatever the recruiter
told you to prepare, and put those first.

Always include the two that expose the most:

- "What did you deliberately not build?"
- "Tell me about a decision that was wrong, and what it cost."

## C. Session shape

Six to eight questions, roughly 45 minutes, recorded. Do not stop to fix an
answer mid-session; the point is to see the habit under pressure, not to produce
one good answer.

## D. Ask for this debrief at the end, verbatim

> For each answer: the duration, whether beat 1 (principle) and beat 2
> (customer) were present in the first 30 seconds, whether the rejected option
> was named unprompted, and whether the question I actually asked was the
> question you answered first.
>
> Then grade A to F on the seven dimensions in the feedback log: opening,
> compression, question fidelity, decision visibility, calibration, questions
> asked, signals received. Name the specific moment behind each grade.
>
> **Verdict:** if this had been the real call, what level would you have read me
> at, and what one sentence would you have written in the debrief.

Paste that output back into the session. It becomes a feedback-log entry, and
any story-specific fixes get written into the story bank from there.
